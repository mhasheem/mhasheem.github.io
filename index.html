Research interests

Agent Memory·Long-Horizon Reasoning·Human-AI Interaction

Building LLM agents with long-horizon memory that stay reliable as knowledge changes and keep people in charge of the decisions that matter.

I develop memory for LLM agents that work over weeks and months, tracing where each piece of knowledge came from, how it has changed, and which earlier decisions still hold when it does. My research combines agent memory, provenance tracking, long-horizon reasoning and evaluation benchmarks with human-centered studies of how people delegate work to AI, so that agents not only remember correctly but also respect who holds authority over their outputs. I also make these agents run dependably on small, private language models for settings such as hospitals and classrooms.

I'm an Associate AI Researcher at Percevio Labs and a BS Computer Science graduate of FAST-NUCES Karachi, where my thesis on small models for multi-agent medical AI grew into a collaboration with the original mAIstro authors, Dr. Michail E. Klontzas and Dr. Eleftherios Tzanis.

I am advised by Dr. Muhammad Usama Saleem, Research Scientist at Amazon, and Dr. Ayman Ali, Assistant Professor at Riyadh College of Technology for Information and Telecommunications.

Research Focus

My research spans two connected areas: long-horizon memory that keeps LLM agents' decisions valid as knowledge changes, and human authority over the work AI produces, with collaborators from Amazon, Duke University and Riyadh College of Technology for Information and Telecommunications.

Long-Horizon Memory for LLM Agents

Agent Memory·Long-Horizon Reasoning·Benchmarks

When an instructor corrects course content, today's AI tools do not carry the fix to the quizzes, grades and feedback that depend on it. EduMem connects instructional evidence, evolving artifacts and authorized decisions through three modules: OriginMem traces artifacts to their evidence, VersionMem keeps versions and approvals, and ReplayMem reconstructs past decisions to preserve, revise, or ask the instructor when authorization is unclear. EduMem-Bench evaluates this over whole semesters, from artifact generation to grading and appeals.

Human Authority in AI-Assisted Education

Human-AI Interaction·Mixed Methods·Accountability

Having a human in the loop does not say what that human is responsible for. Through TRACE, a course-grounded prototype I built, and a cross-cultural study of instructors and students, we found that people delegate work to AI but not authority over it, and that students care about the evidence behind a grade, not just its correctness. The resulting Coursework Authorization Model separates what AI may use as evidence, which outputs become part of a course, and when they may affect students.
